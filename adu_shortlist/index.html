ADU Shortlist

Every for-sale home between $300k and $950k that I found with a second unit already built, in the Denver, Fort Collins, Boulder, Salt Lake and Seattle areas (Salt Lake includes south Davis County and Eden). Listings that only said a unit “could be” added were left out. Each home is scored on its neighborhood, with the same seven criteria and colors as the atlas; drag the weights and the scores and ranks recalculate.

    How to read it

    • Evidence. “Permit on file” means a city or county permit for the second unit shows in the listing’s public permit history. “Claimed legal” means the listing says the unit is legal, permitted, approved or registered, or the county records the property as two or more units; neither is proof. “Described” means the listing states a kitchen, a bathroom and its own entrance (or the unit is a separate building). “Thin” means at least one of those is missing.
    • Score and rank. The score is the neighborhood’s, not the house’s. Rank compares it with every neighborhood in the same atlas area that passes the budget and airport filters, so a listing can outrank the area’s own best-three average.
    • Placement. Homes are placed in a neighborhood from the listing’s map position. The first Denver and Salt Lake City listings were placed from street addresses by hand and can land in the next neighborhood over when they sit near a boundary.
    • Prices and status are as read on October 7–8, 2026, mostly from Redfin. Listings sell and prices drop, so open the link before relying on a row.
    • Before an offer, ask the seller for the permit and the certificate of occupancy for the unit, and check the city’s or county’s own permit records.
